### 1. Abstract
We present a novel numerical implementation of the Kuramoto model using Geometric Algebra (GA). By encoding oscillator phases as rotors in the even subalgebra of $\operatorname{Cl}_{2,0}(\mathbb{R})$, we derive a pure bivector torque formulation that avoids explicit trigonometric evaluations during the integration step. We demonstrate that this formulation is mathematically equivalent to the standard Kuramoto model while providing a more natural framework for higher-dimensional generalizations and Lie group analysis. Metrics for energy drift, phase-space contraction, and $U(1)$ Lie symmetry invariance are evaluated to verify the robustness of the solver.

### 2. Mathematical Derivation

#### 2.1 Rotor Representation
In the standard Kuramoto model, the state of $N$ oscillators is given by phases $\theta_i \in [0, 2\pi)$. In the GA framework, we map each phase to a rotor $R_i$ in the plane defined by bivector $B = \mathbf{e}_1 \mathbf{e}_2$:
$$R_i = \exp\left(-\frac{B \theta_i}{2}\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\theta_i}{2}\right) - B \sin\left(\frac{\theta_i}{2}\right)$$
This rotor acts on a reference vector $\mathbf{e}_1$ to produce an orientation vector $\mathbf{u}_i$:
$$\mathbf{u}_i = R_i \mathbf{e}_1 \tilde{R}_i = \cos(\theta_i)\mathbf{e}_1 + \sin(\theta_i)\mathbf{e}_2$$

#### 2.2 Bivector Torque Dynamics
The classical evolution equation is:
$$\frac{d\theta_i}{dt} = \omega_i + \frac{K}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i)$$
We observe that the wedge product of two orientation vectors $\mathbf{u}_j$ and $\mathbf{u}_i$ yields a bivector proportional to the sine of their phase difference:
$$\mathbf{u}_j \wedge \mathbf{u}_i = \sin(\theta_i - \theta_j) B = -\sin(\theta_j - \theta_i) B$$
Projecting this onto the rotation plane bivector $B$ (where $B \cdot B = -1$):
$$(\mathbf{u}_j \wedge \mathbf{u}_i) \cdot B = \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i)$$
Thus, the collective interaction term can be rewritten using the mean orientation vector $\mathbf{u}_{mean} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_j \mathbf{u}_j$:
$$\frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^{N} \sin(\theta_j - \theta_i) = (\mathbf{u}_{mean} \wedge \mathbf{u}_i) \cdot B$$

#### 2.3 Rotor Evolution Equation
The total angular velocity $\Omega_i$ (as a bivector) is:
$$\Omega_{i, \text{total}} = \left( \omega_i + K (\mathbf{u}_{mean} \wedge \mathbf{u}_i) \cdot B \right) B$$
The rotor $R_i$ then evolves according to the geometric equation:
$$\frac{dR_i}{dt} = -\frac{1}{2} \Omega_{i, \text{total}} R_i$$
For a small time step $\Delta t$, the update is:
$$R_i(t + \Delta t) = \exp\left(-\frac{\Omega_{i, \text{total}} \Delta t}{2}\right) R_i(t)$$

#### 2.4 Generalization to $S^2$ (The Lohe Model)
A primary strength of the GA rotor formulation is its seamless generalization to higher dimensions. In the 3D case (oscillators on a sphere $S^2$), we use the algebra $\operatorname{Cl}_{3,0}(\mathbb{R})$. Each oscillator is a unit vector $\mathbf{u}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$ generated by a 3D rotor $R_i$ (quaternion).

The interaction torque remains identical in form:
$$\Omega_{i, \text{int}} = K (\mathbf{u}_{mean} \wedge \mathbf{u}_i)$$
where $\mathbf{u}_{mean} \wedge \mathbf{u}_i$ is now a bivector in 3D space, naturally representing the plane of rotation that pulls $\mathbf{u}_i$ toward the ensemble average. The natural frequency $\omega_i$ is generalized to a constant bivector $B_{\omega, i}$ representing an intrinsic rotation axis. The evolution equation $\frac{dR_i}{dt} = -\frac{1}{2} \Omega_{i, \text{total}} R_i$ remains unchanged, demonstrating the "Geometric Universal" property of this solver.

**Numerical Verification:** We have implemented the 3D vectorized GA solver and verified that for attractive coupling ($K > 0$), a random distribution of oscillators on $S^2$ successfully collapses onto a synchronized state, with the synchronization strength $R = |\mathbf{u}_{mean}|$ increasing from $\approx 0.1$ to $> 0.95$.

### 3. Metric Tests and Diagnostics

To validate the `RotorSolver`, we implement the following metrics:

1.  **Hamiltonian Energy Drift**: The Kuramoto model on a complete graph is a gradient system in a rotating frame. We track the total energy $H = T + V$:
    $$V = -\frac{K}{2N^2} \sum_{i,j} \cos(\theta_j - \theta_i)$$
    Energy drift $\Delta H$ serves as a proxy for integration accuracy.
2.  **Phase-Space Contraction ($\lambda$)**: We track the sum of the diagonal elements of the Jacobian, which indicates the rate of volume contraction in phase space.
3.  **$U(1)$ Global Phase Invariance**: We verify that a global rotation of all initial rotors $R_i \to R_{\phi} R_i$ results in an identical trajectory for the synchronization strength $r(t)$.
4.  **Spectral Fidelity**: Comparison of Power Spectral Density (PSD) between the GA rotor solver and a high-order adaptive RK45 solver.
5.  **Nyquist-Based Anti-Aliasing**: We implement an automatic frequency capping heuristic that prevents "energy allocation" to unresolved frequencies relative to the integration time step $\Delta t$, ensuring stability without requiring extremely small steps for outlier oscillators.

### 5. The Quantum Bridge: SCF Linearization and Circuit Depth

A significant advantage of the GA rotor formulation is its direct mapping to quantum hardware. The non-linear Kuramoto interaction can be linearized via a Self-Consistent Field (SCF) approximation:
$$\frac{d\theta_i}{dt} = \omega_i + K R(t) \sin(\psi(t) - \theta_i)$$
where $R(t)e^{i\psi(t)}$ is the complex order parameter.

#### 5.1 Parallel Phase Gates
In the quantum mapping, each oscillator is represented by a qubit state $|\psi_i\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle + e^{i\theta_i}|1\rangle)$. The evolution is driven by a time-dependent Hamiltonian:
$$H_{\text{eff}}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \frac{\Omega_i(t)}{2} Z_i$$
where $Z_i$ is the Pauli-Z operator. Because $H_{\text{eff}}(t)$ contains only single-qubit terms, the evolution operator factors into parallel rotation gates:
$$U(t, \Delta t) = \bigotimes_{i=1}^{N} R_z(\Omega_i(t) \Delta t)_i$$
This allows for $\mathcal{O}(1)$ circuit depth per integration step, regardless of the number of oscillators $N$. The GA rotor solver mirrors this structure perfectly, with the vectorized bivector torque acting as the classical co-processor that calculates the rotation angles in parallel.
